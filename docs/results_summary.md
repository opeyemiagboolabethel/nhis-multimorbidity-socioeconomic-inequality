# Results Summary

## Analytical population

The 2024 NHIS Sample Adult public-use file contained 32,629 respondents. The multimorbidity outcome was complete for 32,294 respondents. Survey weights represented approximately 259.7 million U.S. adults.

## Overall burden

Survey-weighted multimorbidity prevalence was **37.7%** (95% CI: **36.9%–38.5%**). The weighted mean chronic-condition count was **1.37** (95% CI: **1.34–1.39**).

## Education and age

Multimorbidity prevalence increased sharply with age and was socially patterned by education, especially from midlife onward.

| Age group | Bachelor's+ | Less than high school | Absolute gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 18–34 | 20.0% | 23.4% | 3.4 pp |
| 35–49 | 22.8% | 26.7% | 3.9 pp |
| 50–64 | 34.9% | 51.9% | 17.0 pp |
| 65+ | 61.7% | 72.5% | 10.8 pp |

The joint Wald test for the education × age interaction was statistically significant (**F = 5.06; p = 0.00000136**), supporting the conclusion that the education gradient differs across age groups.

## Main-effects model

In the survey-weighted full main-effects logistic model, compared with adults with a bachelor's degree or higher:

- some college/associate degree: **OR 1.32** (95% CI: 1.22–1.43)
- high school graduate: **OR 1.26** (95% CI: 1.16–1.37)
- less than high school: **OR 1.73** (95% CI: 1.55–1.92)

Age was the strongest measured correlate. Relative to ages 18–34, adjusted odds were **2.67** times higher at ages 50–64 and **6.48** times higher at ages 65+.

Women had higher adjusted odds than men (**OR 1.39**, 95% CI: 1.30–1.47) under the primary 12-condition definition.

## Poverty and multiple imputation

Using all 10 CDC income imputations and pooling estimates with Rubin's rules, a clear income gradient remained after adjustment for education, age, sex, race/ethnicity, marital status and region. Compared with adults at 400%+ FPL:

- 200–399% FPL: **OR 1.33** (95% CI: 1.23–1.45)
- 100–199% FPL: **OR 1.63** (95% CI: 1.48–1.79)
- <100% FPL: **OR 2.08** (95% CI: 1.85–2.35)

## Condition composition across adulthood

The age profile differed substantially by condition. Hypertension increased from approximately **8%** among adults aged 18–34 to **62%** among those aged 65+. Arthritis rose from **3%** to **48%**, cancer from **1%** to **27%**, and heart disease from **1%** to **19%**. In contrast, diagnosed anxiety and depression were more common in younger adults than in older groups.

This compositional shift is important because an overall multimorbidity count can conceal very different disease profiles across adulthood.

## Interpretation cautions

The analysis is cross-sectional. The interaction between education and age describes age-patterned inequality in 2024, not within-person accumulation over the life course. The race/ethnicity coefficients require particular caution because diagnosis, access to care, reporting and the mix of included conditions may contribute to observed adjusted differences.
