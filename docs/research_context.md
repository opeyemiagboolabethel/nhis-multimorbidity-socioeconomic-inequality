# Research Context

This project is designed as a quantitative-sociology portfolio study rather than a journal-submission project. The research context is included to make the analytical choices theoretically meaningful rather than purely descriptive.

## Established context

- Multimorbidity is socially patterned, with educational and income disadvantage repeatedly associated with greater chronic-condition burden.
- Age is strongly associated with multimorbidity, but socioeconomic inequalities may differ across stages of adulthood.
- Different multimorbidity definitions can produce different patterns, especially when mental-health conditions are included alongside physical conditions.
- Income missingness is nontrivial in national surveys, so the CDC's multiply imputed income files provide a more defensible approach than complete-case income analysis.

## Portfolio contribution

The project uses current 2024 NHIS data to demonstrate:

- complex-survey analysis
- education × age interaction modelling
- multiple-imputation pooling for poverty
- count and binary outcome modelling
- physical-only sensitivity analysis
- careful distinction between age-patterned cross-sectional inequality and longitudinal life-course change

## Suggested background reading

- Johnson-Lawrence et al. (2017), education, race/ethnicity and multimorbidity among U.S. adults.
- Wagner et al. (2022), life-course socioeconomic conditions and multimorbidity in old age.
- CDC/NCHS 2024 NHIS Survey Description and Income Imputation Technical Documentation.
